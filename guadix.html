Guadix for property buyers

What to know before you view

Guadix is one of Spain's best-known cave-home centres. Its extensive Barrio de las Cuevas sits alongside a historic city with everyday services, giving buyers an unusual choice between cave living and conventional urban property.

The exact cave location matters: access, orientation, ventilation, drainage, outside space and the condition of the façade and chimneys can vary from one property to another. Compare the physical home with its registration, boundaries and documented alterations, and independently verify the legal and energy information before buying.

Village guide

About Guadix

Guadix is one of Spain's best-known cave-house areas, with an historic centre and extensive cave neighbourhoods set against the dramatic landscape north of the Sierra Nevada.

Past & heritage

A little history

Guadix was the Roman colony of Acci, founded in a landscape already occupied in prehistoric and Iberian times. Under Islamic rule it became Wadi Ash, a fortified city dominated by the Alcazaba. After the Christian conquest in 1489, the cathedral and churches transformed the urban centre, while displaced and poorer families expanded the cave quarter in the soft clay hills. Pottery, farming, trade and the railway later shaped the modern city, whose cathedral, fortress and Barrio de las Cuevas reveal its unusually layered past.

Local life

Fiestas & traditions

San Torcuato, Guadix's patron, is celebrated on 15 May with religious and civic events. The late-summer Feria fills the city with music, fairground attractions and evening celebrations. On 9 September Cascamorras returns from Baza, where he has failed to recover the Virgen de la Piedad, and is greeted in another exuberant covering of black paint. San Antón and Semana Santa are also deeply rooted traditions.
