The Complete Guide to Buying a Cave House in Spain

Dreaming of owning a cave house in Spain? Cave living offers character, history, peaceful surroundings and a lifestyle closely connected to the landscape of southern Spain. The Granada Geopark and Altiplano areas include many towns and villages associated with cave living. Buyers often explore Baza, Guadix, Huéscar, Benamaurel, Cúllar, Galera, Castilléjar, Caniles, Zújar and Freila, as well as smaller surrounding communities, where properties range from renovation projects to beautifully restored cave homes.

Buying a cave house deserves careful research. Here are some of the most important things to consider before buying.

1. Choose the right area

Different towns and villages offer very different lifestyles. Consider distance from shops and services, healthcare, schools, road access, internet availability, transport links, the local community, and whether you prefer rural or town living. Do not choose a property only because the photographs look beautiful; spend time exploring the surrounding area too.

Places to explore for a cave home

  • Baza — a larger market town and useful base for everyday services while exploring the surrounding cave-home areas.
  • Guadix — one of Andalucía's best-known cave-house areas, with an established cave neighbourhood and a long tradition of cave living.
  • Huéscar — a historic town serving the northern Granada Altiplano and surrounding rural communities.
  • Benamaurel — well known locally for cave homes and a traditional village atmosphere close to the Baza area.
  • Cúllar — another important cave-home area, with village and countryside properties to explore.
  • Galera — a distinctive historic village where cave homes form an important part of the local landscape.
  • Castilléjar — strongly associated with cave living and attractive to buyers looking for a quieter village setting.
  • Caniles — close to Baza, offering a mix of village life, countryside and access to nearby services.
  • Zújar — near Baza and the Negratín reservoir, with a traditional inland-Andalucía setting.
  • Freila — a smaller community near the Negratín reservoir where buyers may find a more rural pace of life.

These are starting points rather than a complete list. Cave properties can also be found in smaller villages and countryside locations throughout the Granada Altiplano and Guadix area. Visit several locations before deciding which community best suits your needs.

2. Check the property's legal status

Before buying, establish exactly what you are purchasing and have the property's documentation checked. An independent Spanish lawyer can investigate ownership, title, registered property information, debts or charges, and the legal status of buildings and alterations. A Nota Simple from the Property Registry can provide important registered information. With unusual properties such as caves, cortijos and rural homes, do not assume every room, extension or outbuilding is automatically registered or permitted.

3. Don't assume every cave is the same

Some homes are almost entirely excavated into a hillside; others combine cave rooms with conventional construction. Look carefully at ventilation, damp and water management, electrical installations, plumbing, drainage, the land or earth above the cave, access, extensions and overall condition. Consider an appropriate independent inspection if you have concerns.

4. Understand the true purchase cost

The advertised price is not the complete cost of buying. Depending on the transaction, buyers may need to budget for taxes, notary and registration expenses, professional fees and specialist checks. Tax rules and individual circumstances can differ, so obtain current professional advice before calculating your final budget.

5. Consider renovation before buying

A lower-priced cave requiring work can be attractive, but investigate renovation costs before purchase. Traditional cave properties behave differently from conventional buildings. Before significant work, establish whether permissions are required and use appropriately qualified professionals where necessary.

6. Ask about the energy certificate

Ask the seller for the property's energy certificate and rating where one is legally required, or the basis and supporting documentation for any applicable exemption. Cave construction by itself should not be treated as proof of exemption. Buyers should independently confirm the documentation before completion.

7. Check utilities and access

Confirm how water and electricity are supplied and whether systems are mains-connected or private/off-grid. Check practical and legal vehicle access. If the property uses a well, solar installation, septic system or other private infrastructure, investigate its condition and documentation.

8. Visit more than once

If possible, do not base your decision on one viewing. Look beyond decoration and furniture. Check outside areas, access, boundaries and surrounding land as well as the cave itself. A second viewing often reveals things you did not notice the first time.

9. Use independent professional advice

Cave Homes Spain brings property sellers and potential buyers together, but it is not a substitute for independent legal, technical or financial advice. Before purchasing, consider using an independent lawyer and other appropriately qualified professionals to check the property and transaction. The professionals you choose should represent your interests.

10. Think about the lifestyle — and take your time

Are you looking for a permanent home, peaceful rural retreat, renovation project, holiday property or a base for exploring Andalucía? Knowing what you want makes choosing easier. Research the location, investigate the property, understand the paperwork and ask questions before committing.
