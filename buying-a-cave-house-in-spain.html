Buying a Cave House in Spain

Cave homes are distinctive properties, so buyers should investigate both the usual Spanish property checks and issues specific to the individual cave home before committing to a purchase.

Legal and property checks

Use an independent Spanish lawyer to check ownership, title, registered property information, debts or charges and the legal status of buildings and alterations. Do not assume that every room, extension, outbuilding or potential future use is automatically registered or permitted.

Survey and condition

Consider an appropriate independent inspection of the property. For a cave home, ask about the condition of the cave structure, ventilation, drainage, moisture management, roof or earth covering, retaining structures and any conventional built sections.

Energy information

Ask the seller for the property's energy certificate and rating where one is legally required, or the basis and supporting documentation for any applicable exemption. Cave construction by itself should not be treated as proof that a property is exempt.

Water, electricity and access

Confirm how water and electricity are supplied, whether systems are mains-connected or private/off-grid, and the legal and practical arrangements for vehicle access. Where a property uses a well, solar installation, septic system or other private infrastructure, investigate its condition and documentation.

Costs and professional advice

Allow for taxes, legal fees, registration and other purchase costs relevant to your circumstances. Requirements can change and differ by transaction, so obtain current advice from qualified professionals before purchasing.
