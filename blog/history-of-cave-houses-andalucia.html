The History of Cave Houses in Andalucía

Homes carved into the landscape.

Drive through parts of Granada province and you may notice something unusual rising from the hillsides: whitewashed chimneys appearing from the earth. Look more closely and you discover homes beneath them.

Cave houses are one of the most distinctive features of parts of Andalucía. Around places such as Guadix, Baza, Huéscar and the Granada Altiplano, generations of people have created homes by excavating into suitable hillsides and adapting them as their families and needs changed.

Why build a home underground?

Before modern air conditioning and insulation, people worked closely with the landscape around them. In areas where the geology allowed excavation, building into the earth could offer practical advantages. The surrounding ground provides substantial thermal mass, helping to moderate changes in indoor temperature.

Rather than constructing every wall and roof conventionally, rooms could be excavated into the hillside. Over time, this produced a distinctive form of vernacular architecture.

A home that could grow

Traditional cave houses could evolve. Where the structure and conditions permitted, spaces could be adapted or extended as circumstances changed. This helps explain some of the irregular and individual layouts found in older cave properties today.

The landscape itself helped determine the shape of the house.

The unmistakable cave-house chimney

Perhaps the most recognisable feature is above ground. Chimneys rise through the earth covering the cave and contribute to the extraordinary appearance of cave districts in Granada province. Whitewashed entrances and chimneys contrast with the natural colours of the surrounding hills.

From traditional dwellings to modern homes

Cave dwellings have served ordinary communities over many generations, although their history and social context differ from place to place. Some properties continued to be occupied and maintained while others fell out of use.

Many cave dwellings have since been restored and adapted for modern living. Contemporary kitchens and bathrooms, electricity, terraces and other modern features can now sit alongside older excavated rooms. Some properties combine the original cave with conventional construction at the entrance.

Guadix and the Granada Altiplano

Guadix is particularly well known for its cave neighbourhoods, while cave properties are also found across communities of the wider Granada Altiplano and around Baza. They are not simply historical curiosities: many remain homes, while others have been renovated or returned to use.

Preserving something unusual

Every older cave house is different. Renovation therefore deserves care. Ventilation, drainage, moisture management, access and structural conditions should be properly understood, and buyers should investigate a property's legal documentation and obtain appropriate professional advice before substantial work.

A living piece of Andalucía

The older cave homes form part of a building tradition shaped by landscape, geology, climate, available resources and generations of human ingenuity. Today, behind some of those whitewashed entrances and distinctive chimneys, modern family life continues within spaces that reflect a much older way of creating a home.
